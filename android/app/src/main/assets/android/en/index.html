SDR4NOOB for Android

The Android version works with any USB radio receiver (SDR) it has a driver for. Built-in drivers cover receivers based on the RTL2832U chip (for example, RTL-SDR Blog V4/V3 and DVB-T dongles with R820T/R828D, E4000, FC0012/FC0013 tuners), HackRF (One, Jawbreaker, rad1o) and Airspy R2/Mini. You don't need to install a separate driver app.

Connecting the receiver

  1. You need an OTG adapter (USB-C or micro-USB → USB-A) or a USB-C to USB-C cable. On some phones OTG has to be turned on in the settings ("Connected devices" → "OTG").
  2. Plug in the receiver. Android offers to open SDR4NOOB — accept (you can tick "always"). If you opened the app yourself, tap the receiver in the list and allow access to the USB device.
  3. Choose "Radio and spectrum". Tap the spectrum to tune to a station, drag to shift the band, tap the frequency to type it in, "Stations…" offers ready-made bands.
Power. The receiver draws 100–300 mA from the phone, and more with antenna power (bias-T) on. The battery drains noticeably faster; older phones may not supply enough current — an adapter with external power helps then.

Other receivers

  • Network rtl_tcp. The receiver is connected to a computer (or a Raspberry Pi) running rtl_tcp -a 0.0.0.0; in the app choose "Network rtl_tcp…" and enter an address like 192.168.1.10:1234.
  • HackRF and Airspy run at 2 and 2.5 MHz respectively; their native sample rate is converted to the app's view bandwidth (as in the Windows version). HackRF draws noticeably more power from the phone — you need a phone with a good USB port or an adapter with external power.
  • Airspy HF+, SDRplay, LimeSDR, PlutoSDR, FUNcube are recognized over USB, but drivers for them in the Android version are still planned (some of them work in the Windows version).
  • Test signal — a few practice stations without a receiver: FM stereo with RDS on 101.0 MHz, FM on 102.6 and 104.5 MHz, narrow FM on 145.500 and 145.525 MHz, AM on 999 kHz, USB on 14.230 MHz, Morse on 7.030 MHz, DMR on 446.1 MHz.

What this version can do

  • Spectrum and waterfall of the receiver band (up to 2.88 MHz).
  • Radio: FM broadcast with stereo and RDS (station name, radio text), narrow FM (amateur, PMR, LPD), AM (airband, MW), upper and lower sideband (USB/LSB) and Morse (CW) with AGC; squelch, channel bandwidth, gain, bias-T, frequency correction in ppm.
  • DMR (the "DMR digital radio" mode): who is calling whom in each slot, group and private calls, event log, voice. Encrypted calls give no sound. DMR search — the ⋮ menu: the app sweeps a band and shows the channels found (color code CC, voice or beacon, last IDs); tap one to listen.
  • IQ file on the main screen — plays back a recording (your own or made with SDR4NOOB, SDR#, rtl_sdr on a PC).
  • SSTV (main screen): pictures from the ISS (145.800 MHz, ARISS sessions) and from amateurs on HF (14.230 MHz USB) — Robot, Martin, Scottie, PD, Wraase. The mode is detected from the VIS code; if the start was missed, pick the mode and press "Start now". Pictures are saved to "Pictures/SDR4NOOB/SSTV" (on Android 9 and older — to the app folder Android/data/…/files/Pictures, the path is shown after saving); a WAV file can be decoded as well.
  • APRS: amateur stations on a map (144.800 MHz) — positions, objects, weather, messages; packet log.
  • ADS-B aircraft: aircraft on a map from 1090 MHz signals — callsign, altitude, speed, heading, country and airline. You need a 1090 MHz antenna (a 6.9 cm whip will do); in a city you usually hear 100–250 km. The OpenStreetMap map is downloaded from the internet and cached on the phone; set the receiver location with "Home = map center".
  • TV: a DVB-T/T2 digital multiplex meter (the whole 8 MHz channel is assembled from several retunes: C/N, level, ripple, OFDM mode and guard interval), an analog picture (black and white: the receiver bandwidth is narrower than the video signal) and a channel scan over the channel plans of Russia, Europe, the UK and the USA.
  • Satellites: positions and ground tracks on a map, passes over you (orbits from celestrak TLEs — internet needed at least once), choice of satellite and transmitter; the frequency is corrected for Doppler automatically. Meteor-M (137.9 MHz): LRPT image reception — "Image" shows the picture, "Save image" puts it into "Pictures/SDR4NOOB/Метеор". Meteor needs a 137 MHz antenna (QFH or a V-dipole) and an open sky.
  • Settings (main screen): your own data folder, e.g. on a memory card — recordings and images then go there, into an SDR4NOOB subfolder; receiver location; update check; clearing the map and orbit cache.
  • My frequencies — your own station list in groups; import and export to CSV (the same file opens in SDR4NOOB on a PC and in Excel) and to SDR# XML.
  • Recording of audio (the "● Audio to WAV" button) and IQ (⋮ menu → "IQ recording") as 16-bit WAV — to "Download/SDR4NOOB/Звук". IQ takes a lot of space: at 1.2 MHz bandwidth about 290 MB per minute. IQ files open in SDR4NOOB on a PC and in SDR#. If the phone storage can't keep up with the IQ stream, recording stops (rather than gluing parts with gaps) — reduce the view bandwidth. While recording, the file is not visible in "Download"; if the app crashed, the unfinished recording stays hidden and Android deletes it by itself after a week.
  • Background reception: you can turn the screen off or switch to another app — the sound keeps playing. The notification shade shows the frequency and a "Stop" button. To close the radio, press "Back".
  • The beginner's guide — the same as in the Windows version, including the interactive "Radio signal path" diagram.
  • Interface language — Russian or English: "Language" on the main screen (by default — as in the system).
  • Update check: when a new version is released, a link to the APK appears on the main screen.
